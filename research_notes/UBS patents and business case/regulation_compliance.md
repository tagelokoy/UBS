# Regulation and compliance landscape for UBS cells, Anodyne chargers and cell-less devices (Norway/EEA and EU), as of 7 October 2026

Scope note: UBS = proposed open standard for user-replaceable protected Li-ion cells in 14500/18650/21700 sizes, each with an NFC chip and a fuel gauge. Anodyne = brand selling such cells, chargers, and devices that ship without cells, plus a proposed EUR 3 return credit per returned cell. Research was done on 7 Oct 2026. Primary legal text was read directly from the Official Journal version of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 (Publications Office / EUR-Lex). Some 2026 developments rest on secondary sources only, and that is flagged where it applies.

## 1. EU Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542: requirements and dates that apply to UBS and Anodyne

### Takeaway
The Battery Regulation mostly helps the idea. From **18 February 2027**, Article 11 requires portable batteries in consumer products to be removable and replaceable by the end user with commercially available tools. It also bans using software to block a "compatible" replacement battery and requires spare batteries to stay available for 5 years. That is close to the UBS thesis written into law. The heavy obligations (battery passport, carbon footprint, recycled content, performance minimums) do **not** apply to portable batteries. The obligations that do apply to Anodyne as a cell seller are CE marking with self-declared conformity (Module A, already in force), the collection symbol (in force), the harmonised label with capacity (from 18 Aug 2026 at the earliest, and likely later because the implementing act is late), a QR code on every battery (18 Feb 2027), and producer registration plus EPR in **every** country where it first makes cells available (in force in the EU since 18 Aug 2025).

### Cited Findings

**Application dates and structure**
- The Regulation entered into force on 17 Aug 2023 and applies from 18 Feb 2024, with exceptions: Article 11 (removability) applies from **18 Feb 2027**; Article 17 (conformity assessment) and Chapter VI (notified bodies) apply from **18 Aug 2024**; Chapter VIII (end-of-life management, i.e. EPR, collection and treatment) applies from **18 Aug 2025** (Art. 96(2)). — [Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj); [Commission batteries page](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/waste-and-recycling/batteries_en)
- A consolidated version dated 31 Jul 2025 exists and incorporates the due diligence postponement. — [EUR-Lex consolidated 02023R1542-20250731](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/2025-07-31/eng)

**Definitions that matter for UBS**
- A "portable battery of general use" is defined as a portable battery "specifically designed to be interoperable and that has one of the following common formats 4,5 Volts (3R12), button cell, D, C, AA, AAA, AAAA, A23, 9 Volts (PP3)" (Art. 3(10)). **14500/18650/21700 are not on the list**, so UBS cells would be ordinary "portable batteries", not "portable batteries of general use". — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Portable batteries of general use (excluding button cells) must meet minimum performance and durability values from 18 Aug 2028, or 24 months after the delegated act, whichever is later (Art. 9). By 31 Dec 2030 the Commission must assess phasing out *non-rechargeable* portable batteries of general use (Art. 9(3)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- A portable battery is sealed, weighs 5 kg or less, is not designed for industrial use and is not an EV, LMT or SLI battery. There is no de minimis. — [EcoComply summary](https://ecocomply.ai/blog/eu-batteries-regulation-removability-2027)

**Article 11: removability and replaceability (from 18 Feb 2027)**
- Art. 11(1): anyone placing on the market "products incorporating portable batteries shall ensure that those batteries are readily removable and replaceable by the end-user at any time during the lifetime of the product". This applies "only … to entire batteries and not to individual cells". — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 11](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- "Readily removable by the end-user" means removable "with the use of commercially available tools, without requiring the use of specialised tools, unless provided free of charge with the product, proprietary tools, thermal energy, or solvents to disassemble the product". Products must come with instructions and safety information on use, removal and replacement, and those must also be "available permanently online, on a publicly available website". — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 11(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Art. 11(6): "readily replaceable" means the battery "can be substituted by another compatible battery without affecting the functioning, the performance or the safety" of the appliance. — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 11(6)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Art. 11(7): batteries must be available as spare parts "for a minimum of five years after placing the last unit of the equipment model on the market, with a reasonable and non-discriminatory price for independent professionals and end-users". — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 11(7)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Art. 11(8): "Software shall not be used to impede the replacement of a portable battery … with another compatible battery or key components." — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 11(8)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Art. 11(2) derogations, under which a battery need only be replaceable by "independent professionals": (a) appliances designed to work mainly in splashing water, water streams or immersion and intended to be washable or rinseable (only where needed for safety); (b) professional medical imaging/radiotherapy and IVD devices. Art. 11(3): no obligation where continuity of power and a permanent connection are needed for safety, or for data integrity in products whose main function is collecting data. Art. 11(4) lets the Commission add exemptions by delegated act. — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 11(2)-(4)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- LMT batteries (e-bikes, e-scooters) are stricter: the batteries **and the individual cells** must be replaceable by an independent professional (Art. 11(5)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 11(5)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- The Commission published Art. 11 guidelines as Commission Notice C/2025/214. They say the end user is an adult with no specific experience or qualifications. — [Commission Notice C/2025/214, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=OJ%3AC_202500214); [CDX summary](https://public.cdxsystem.com/en/web/cdx/w/battery-regulation-new-guidelines-on-removability-requirements)
- **2026 development (secondary source only):** on 14 Jul 2026 the Commission reportedly adopted delegated act C(2026) 5031, which would expand the Art. 11(2) derogations from 2 to 8 categories: wet appliances incl. wearables, medical, small or sealed wearables, electric toys until 31 Jul 2030, wireless food probes, ATEX products, on-body drug delivery, roof-mounted agricultural/construction telematics. Revised guidelines C(2026) 5032 came with it. As of 22 Aug 2026 it was reportedly in Parliament/Council scrutiny and not yet in the OJ. The same source says the guidelines use EN 45554:2020 as the reference method. — [EcoComply](https://ecocomply.ai/blog/eu-batteries-regulation-removability-2027); [Dudkowiak](https://www.dudkowiak.com/blog/battery-replacement-and-removability-starting-in-2027-new-guidelines-from-the-european-commission). *I could not verify this on EUR-Lex.*

**Labelling, marking and QR code (Art. 13)**
- From **18 Aug 2025**, all batteries must carry the separate collection symbol (crossed-out wheelie bin). For cylindrical cells it must cover at least 1.5% of the surface area, max 5×5 cm. If it would be smaller than 0.47×0.47 cm, a symbol of at least 1×1 cm goes on the packaging instead (Art. 13(4)). Cd/Pb symbols apply above 0.002% Cd or 0.004% Pb (Art. 13(5)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- General-information label (Annex VI Part A) and, for **rechargeable portable batteries, a capacity label**: from "18 August 2026 or 18 months after the date of entry into force of the implementing act referred to in paragraph 10, whichever is the latest" (Art. 13(1)-(2)). The Commission was due to adopt that implementing act by 18 Aug 2025 (Art. 13(10)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Status of the labelling implementing act: a draft was released in Dec 2025, with feedback until 26 Jan 2026 and adoption expected in Q2 2026. A compliance vendor wrote on 18 Aug 2026 that "we have not been able to confirm that the implementing act has been published in the Official Journal". The effective label date therefore slips to 18 months after it enters into force (roughly 2027-28). — [Pryor Technology, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.pryormarking.com/18-august-2026-and-the-eu-battery-regulation/); [Certivo](https://www.certivo.com/blog-details/eu-battery-regulation-labelling-2026-capacity-chemistry-and-hazardous-substance-rules)
- From **18 Feb 2027**, all batteries must carry a QR code. For portable batteries it must link to the label information, the EU declaration of conformity, the due diligence report (where applicable), and the waste prevention/management information in Art. 74(1)(a)-(f). Labels and QR code must be printed or engraved on the battery. If that is not possible because of size, they go on packaging and accompanying documents (Art. 13(6)-(7)). The Commission may allow "alternative types of smart labels" in place of or alongside the QR code by delegated act (Art. 13(8)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 13(6)-(8)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Re-used, repurposed or remanufactured batteries must be relabelled with information on their change of status (Art. 13(9)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 13(9)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)

**CE marking and conformity assessment**
- For batteries made in series, conformity with Articles 6, 9, 10, 12, 13 and 14 is assessed under **Module A (internal production control)** or Module D1 (Art. 17(1)). Notified-body modules are needed only for Arts. 7-8 (carbon footprint and recycled content), which do not apply to portable batteries. — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- CE marking on batteries has been required since 18 Aug 2024. — [Pryor Technology](https://www.pryormarking.com/18-august-2026-and-the-eu-battery-regulation/)
- An economic operator established in the EU (manufacturer, importer, authorised representative or fulfilment service provider) is required (market surveillance, Reg. 2019/1020 as amended by Art. 91). — [EcoComply](https://ecocomply.ai/blog/eu-batteries-regulation-removability-2027)

**Battery passport and carbon footprint: portable batteries are out of scope**
- Battery passport: from 18 Feb 2027 it applies to "each LMT battery, each industrial battery with a capacity greater than 2 kWh and each electric vehicle battery" (Art. 77(1)). It does not apply to portable batteries. Passport data must use "open standards … interoperable format … without vendor lock-in" (Art. 77(5)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 77](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Carbon footprint declaration: applies only to EV batteries, rechargeable industrial batteries >2 kWh and LMT batteries (Art. 7(1)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 7](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Performance/durability documents (Art. 10) apply only to industrial >2 kWh, LMT and EV batteries. — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 10](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)

**EPR and producer registration**
- Every Member State keeps a producer register. Producers must "submit an application for registration in each Member State where they make a battery available on the market for the first time", and may only make batteries available (including batteries **incorporated in appliances**) if they or their EPR authorised representative are registered there (Art. 55(1)-(2)). Registration includes details of the appointed PRO (Art. 55(3)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 55](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Producers selling directly to end users in another Member State must appoint an EPR authorised representative in each such state (Art. 56(3)). This has applied since 18 Aug 2025. — [EcoComply](https://ecocomply.ai/blog/eu-batteries-regulation-removability-2027)
- Producers may appoint a PRO, and Member States may make that mandatory (Art. 57(1)). PRO fees must avoid "disproportionate burden on producers of small quantities … including SMEs". Fees must be **modulated "as a minimum by battery category and battery chemistry, taking into account as appropriate the rechargeability, the level of recycled content … whether the batteries were subject to preparation for re-use … repurposing … remanufacturing, and their carbon footprint"** (Art. 57). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 57](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Distributors, **including distance sellers**, must take back waste portable batteries free of charge with no purchase obligation. Distance sellers must "provide for a sufficient number of collection points" (Art. 62(1), (4)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 62](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)

**Collection and recycling targets** (obligations on producers/PROs, which flow into PRO fees)
- Portable battery collection targets: **45% by 31 Dec 2023; 63% by 31 Dec 2027; 73% by 31 Dec 2030** (Art. 59(3)). End users must not be charged to discard waste portable batteries (Art. 59(4)). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 59](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Recycling efficiency for lithium-based batteries: **65% by weight by 31 Dec 2025, 70% by 31 Dec 2030**. Material recovery by 31 Dec 2027: 90% cobalt, 90% copper, 90% lead, **50% lithium**, 90% nickel (Annex XII Parts B-C). The 2031 tier (80% lithium, 95% Co/Ni/Cu) was not extracted verbatim. — [Reg. 2023/1542 Annex XII](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Treatment rules require waste lithium batteries to be protected from heat, water and crushing during storage (Annex XII Part A). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Annex XII](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)

**Due diligence (raw-material supply chain)**
- Chapter VII does not apply to economic operators with net turnover below **EUR 40 million** that are not part of a larger group (Art. 47). — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 47](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- The application date was moved from 18 Aug 2025 to **18 Aug 2027** by Regulation (EU) 2025/1561 (Omnibus IV), in force 31 Jul 2025. Commission due diligence guidelines are now due by 26 Jul 2026. — [CDX](https://public.cdxsystem.com/en/web/cdx/w/eu-postpones-battery-regulation-due-diligence-obligations-to-2027); [Minespider](https://www.minespider.com/blog/eu-battery-due-diligence-postponed-what-it-means-for-companies); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/european-commission-unveils-two-proposals-impacting-the-eu-batteries-regulation)
- A separate Omnibus IV proposal (a "small mid-cap" category) would raise the exemption to **EUR 150 million turnover** and move due diligence reporting to every three years. It needed Parliament and Council approval, and I did not confirm its adoption. — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/european-commission-unveils-two-proposals-impacting-the-eu-batteries-regulation)
- Labelling, waste management and EPR were explicitly **not** delayed by the Omnibus IV due diligence postponement. — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/european-commission-unveils-two-proposals-impacting-the-eu-batteries-regulation)

**Review clauses relevant to UBS**
- By 31 Dec 2027 the Commission must assess deposit return systems for batteries, "in particular for portable batteries of general use" (Art. 63). See section 3. — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 63](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- By 1 Jan 2025 the Commission had to assess "how best to introduce harmonised standards for a common charger for … rechargeable batteries incorporated into specific categories of electrical and electronic equipment covered by Directive 2012/19/EU" and for LMT batteries (Art. 94(6)). Recital 41: "interoperability of chargers for specific categories of batteries could reduce unnecessary waste and costs". — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 94(6), recital 41](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)

### Inferences
- **Biggest tailwind:** Art. 11 makes every consumer-device maker in the EU/EEA design for user-replaceable batteries from Feb 2027, ban parts-pairing (11(8)), and stock spares for 5+ years (11(7)). UBS offers an off-the-shelf way to comply: a standard, available, "compatible battery" per 11(6). The open-standard pitch to OEMs could be "Article 11 compliance in a box".
- **Definition gap:** because 18650/21700/14500 are not "portable batteries of general use", UBS cells avoid the Art. 9 performance minimums (2028+) and any phase-out assessment. They also don't benefit from "general use" status, which would be the natural route to future EU interoperability measures. A long-term policy goal for UBS could be to lobby for adding standard Li-ion cylindrical formats to Art. 3(10). That would bring obligations (Art. 9 minimum performance) as well as legitimacy.
- Note that Art. 11(1) applies to "entire batteries and not to individual cells". If a UBS device uses a multi-cell holder, the "battery" for Art. 11 purposes may be the whole holder or pack, not the individual cell. **Legal check needed** on how a device that takes loose cells is characterised.
- The NFC chip + fuel gauge fits well with the QR code/information duties (Art. 13(6)) and with the "smart label" delegated power (Art. 13(8)). It is not required by law for portable batteries. The cell-level state-of-health data could support "preparation for re-use" claims and EPR fee modulation (Art. 57).
- Cost and risk drivers: per-country registration plus a PRO in each market (or an authorised representative per Member State for direct sales). Labels and QR on a small cylinder: the 14500 likely needs packaging fallback for some label elements. Distance-seller take-back obligations. A 5-year spare-parts duty for Anodyne devices (easy, because the spare part is the standard cell).
- Anodyne will be far below the EUR 40m due diligence threshold for years. Due diligence matters only indirectly (cell suppliers may pass on costs).

### Gaps
- Could not confirm on EUR-Lex whether the Art. 13(10) labelling implementing act has been published as of Oct 2026. Secondary sources up to 18 Aug 2026 say no.
- Could not confirm on EUR-Lex that delegated act C(2026) 5031 (Art. 11 derogations) exists or is final. Only blog sources were found.

## 2. Norway/EEA: incorporation of the Battery Regulation and how producer responsibility works today

### Takeaway
As of 7 Oct 2026, Regulation 2023/1542 is **still not incorporated into the EEA Agreement**. The EFTA EEA-Lex factsheet lists it as "under scrutiny", with no Joint Committee Decision. Norway is running on the old regime, **avfallsforskriften chapter 3**, plus EEA product rules. Miljødirektoratet has drafted a new **batteriforskrift** that adopts the Regulation by reference and will take effect only after the Joint Committee decision. In practice a producer must join an **approved producer responsibility organisation** (e.g. Batteriretur, RENAS, Norsirk) before placing batteries on the Norwegian market. Registration in the national producer register runs through that organisation.

### Cited Findings
- EFTA EEA-Lex: Regulation 2023/1542 is "EU legal act marked as EEA relevant by the EU and under scrutiny for incorporation into the EEA Agreement by Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway". No JCD is listed (checked 7 Oct 2026). — [EFTA EEA-Lex factsheet 32023R1542](https://www.efta.int/eea-lex/32023r1542)
- RENAS (2 Oct 2025): the Regulation "is not yet applicable in Norway". Implementation requires EEA incorporation and a new Norwegian battery regulation (batteriforskrift). Avfallsforskriften chapter 3 remains in force. Miljødirektoratet said incorporation was "expected in the near future". RENAS has applied for approval as a PRO for four battery categories (portable, LMT, industrial, SLI) and partners with Batteriretur for EV batteries. — [RENAS](https://renas.no/aktuelt/siste-nytt/status-for-eus-nye-batteriforordning-i-norge/)
- The draft batteriforskrift implements most of the Regulation by reference. It will not apply before EEA incorporation, which had earlier been expected in spring 2025. — [Miljødirektoratet høringsnotat](https://hoering.miljodirektoratet.no/Hoering/LastNedVedlegg/29904); [Miljødirektoratet batteriveileder](https://www.miljodirektoratet.no/ansvarsomrader/avfall/for-naringsliv/batteriveilederen/)
- Miljødirektoratet held a national consultation in June 2025 on amending the draft batteriforskrift to reflect the due diligence postponement. — [Miljødirektoratet høring juni 2025](https://www.miljodirektoratet.no/hoeringer/2025/juni-2025/nasjonal-horing---endring-av-batteriforordningen-og-endring-av-foreslatt-batteriforskrift---behorig-aktsomhet-due-diligence/)
- A further consultation (ref. 2026/4456, published 27 Apr 2026, deadline 11 May 2026) proposed (1) clarifying which EPR rules apply to batteries already on the Norwegian market before the forskrift enters into force, and (2) implementing an EU amendment on a product passport register for unique battery identifiers. — [Miljødirektoratet høring april 2026](https://www.miljodirektoratet.no/hoeringer/2026/april-2026/horing-av-endringer-i-batteriforskriften/)
- A Norwegian EEA memo (May 2026), "Batteri – Forenklingspakke miljø: batteriforordningen og industrirapporteringsportalen", reportedly assesses Commission proposal 2025/981 as EEA-relevant and acceptable, and says the amendments will require changes to the proposed batteriforskrift. *Page not readable (403). Content taken from a search snippet.* — [Regjeringen EØS-notat](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/sub/eos-notatbasen/notatene/2026/mai/batteri-forenklingspakke-miljo-batteriforordningen-og-industrirapporteringsportalen/id3171866/)
- The current Norwegian battery rules are in avfallsforskriften chapter 3 (Kasserte batterier). — [Lovdata avfallsforskriften kap. 3](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2004-06-01-930/KAPITTEL_3)
- Who is a "producer" under Miljødirektoratet's guidance: manufacturers ("design, produce batteries or get a battery produced"), importers from outside the EEA, those first making EEA-purchased batteries available in Norway, and those reselling used batteries (except direct re-use for the same purpose). All producers must "be member of an approved producer responsibility company for batteries before the battery is brought into circulation". Approved organisations handle registration in the national producer responsibility register and the collection and treatment of waste batteries. — [Miljødirektoratet: Hvilken rolle og hvilke plikter har du?](https://www.miljodirektoratet.no/ansvarsomrader/avfall/for-naringsliv/batteriveilederen/hvilken-rolle-og-hvilke-plikter-har-du/)
- Under the new regime more actors become producers, including re-use operators, who must join an approved PRO. The Regulation's term "producer responsibility organisation" replaces the older Norwegian "returselskap"/"retursystem". — [Miljødirektoratet høringsnotat](https://hoering.miljodirektoratet.no/Hoering/LastNedVedlegg/29904)
- Norsirk says it handles all battery categories. — [Norsirk](https://aktuelt.norsirk.no/alle-batterikategorier)
- AS Batteriretur was set up on 22 Dec 1993 on the initiative of environment minister Thorbjørn Berntsen, as a nationwide non-profit ("ideelt") battery collection and recycling company. — [Batteriretur seminar slides 2017 (via Grønt Punkt)](https://grontpunkt.no/media/0cueq2xs/batteriretur-frukostseminar-17-oktober-2017.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a Norway-first launch in 2026-27, Anodyne must join an approved battery PRO before its first sale (Batteriretur, RENAS and Norsirk are candidates; get quotes from each). It must also join a WEEE/EE PRO for its chargers and devices. Once the batteriforskrift takes effect, EU-style labelling, QR, Art. 11 and other obligations will apply in Norway from the EU dates or from Norwegian entry into force, whichever is later. **Transitional rules are still being consulted on, so a Norwegian environmental lawyer or the chosen PRO should confirm timing.**
- Selling into the EU from Norway means Norway is a "third country" for EU registration. Anodyne needs an EU economic operator / EPR authorised representative in each Member State it sells to directly (see section 1). That is a real fixed cost per country, typically paid to compliance-scheme providers.
- If Anodyne collects returned cells, tests them via NFC state of health, and resells them, it becomes a "producer" of those used batteries in Norway (re-use exception aside). It would need PRO membership for them and new labels under Art. 13(9) once the Regulation applies.

- No confirmed date for the EEA Joint Committee Decision. The EFTA site still says "under scrutiny".
- Did not find a Norwegian deposit (pant) scheme for batteries. Batteriretur's 1993 origin was a producer-funded system, not a consumer deposit. Norway's well-known pant precedents are beverage containers and the car scrapping deposit (vrakpant), but I did not fetch sources for these.

## 3. Return credit (EUR 3 per cell): compatibility with EPR, and deposit-refund precedents

### Takeaway
A voluntary EUR 3 credit fits the Regulation. EPR sets minimum duties: free take-back, collection targets, and fee modulation that rewards re-use. Nothing found forbids a producer from paying end users an extra incentive. Policy is moving toward deposits. Article 63 requires the Commission to assess battery deposit-return systems by 31 Dec 2027, a study is under way in 2026, and Austria, Germany and Lithuania asked in Dec 2025 for it to be sped up because of waste-facility fires. The portable-battery industry association (EPBA) opposes mandatory DRS.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 63: "By 31 December 2027, the Commission shall assess the feasibility and potential benefits of establishment of deposit return systems for batteries, in particular for portable batteries of general use", and report to Parliament and Council. Recital 109: assess DRS "to maximise collection and reduce safety risks", considering "national and harmonised Union-wide" systems. — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 63, recital 109](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj); [EUWID](https://www.euwid-recycling.com/news/policy/feasibility-of-deposit-return-systems-for-batteries-to-be-reviewed-by-end-of-2027-310123/)
- EPR rules bar charging end users to return portable batteries (Art. 59(4)). Distributors and distance sellers must take back free of charge (Art. 62). PRO fees must be modulated for re-use, remanufacturing and recycled content (Art. 57). — [Reg. 2023/1542](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- On 16 Dec 2025, Austria, backed by Germany and Lithuania, urged the Commission to assess an EU-wide battery DRS "without delay". They cited summer 2025 fires causing "damage running into millions of euros" at waste facilities and waste operators' difficulty getting affordable insurance. They also proposed chemistry labelling, consumer education, monitoring of online sales and a producer-financed fire compensation fund. — [EUWID, 16 Dec 2025](https://www.euwid-recycling.com/news/policy/austria-germany-and-lithuania-press-european-commission-for-swift-decision-on-battery-deposit-scheme-151225/)

### Inferences
- **Compatibility:** a voluntary credit sits on top of EPR and does not replace it. Anodyne still has to join a PRO and pay fees. Collected cells should be handed to the PRO or an authorised treatment operator, or formally routed to "preparation for re-use", so they count toward collection reporting. Waste batteries collected by Anodyne are waste and must be transported under waste and dangerous-goods rules (see section 6).
- The credit must not be framed as a fee to return batteries (Art. 59(4)). A credit given at purchase and refunded on return would legally be a deposit. Structure it as a **rebate on return** (for example a voucher or account credit) rather than a surcharge, unless and until a national DRS framework exists. **Get a consumer-law and EPR lawyer to review the mechanics** (price display under consumer pricing rules, VAT treatment of the credit, unclaimed deposits).
- **Help from EPR:** fee modulation for re-use and remanufacturing (Art. 57) means NFC-verified returned cells that are re-certified for re-use could lower PRO fees. That is a direct financial argument for the NFC/fuel-gauge design. If the Commission recommends a DRS in its 2027 report, Anodyne's credit would be an early, compatible version. Unique per-cell IDs (NFC) are exactly what a fraud-resistant battery DRS would need, which answers a core EPBA objection (fraud).
- EUR 3 is small next to the EUR 25 figures floated for e-bike batteries, but large next to the value of an 18650 cell. Unit economics belong in the business-case notes.

## 4. Safety and product compliance (cells, chargers, NFC devices) and typical testing costs

### Takeaway
Cells need **UN 38.3** (transport, mandatory to ship at all) and **IEC/EN 62133-2** (safety, the de facto basis for GPSR conformity and for retailers and marketplaces). Expect roughly **USD 2-7k and 3-6 weeks** for UN 38.3 and **USD 5-12k and 6-12 weeks** for IEC 62133-2 per cell model. Three UBS sizes means three or more cell models, and possibly more if variants with different capacity or chemistry exist (Li-ion vs LiFePO₄). Chargers need LVD (EN 62368-1), EMC, RoHS and REACH declarations, at roughly **USD 4-15k and 5-12 weeks**. Devices or chargers with an NFC reader are radio equipment under RED. The RED cybersecurity delegated act has applied since 1 Aug 2025, will be replaced by the **Cyber Resilience Act**, which applies fully from **11 Dec 2027**. GPSR has applied since 13 Dec 2024.

### Cited Findings
**Cell testing costs and lead times**
- UN 38.3 testing typically costs USD 2,000-7,000 over 3-6 weeks. IEC 62133-2 typically costs USD 5,000-12,000 over 6-10 weeks. — [Ufine Battery certification guide 2026](https://www.ufinebattery.com/blog/essential-guide-to-battery-certification-types-costs-timeframes-and-standards/)
- One US lab lists IEC 62133-2:2017 **single-cell** testing from USD 8,225 with an 8-12 week lead time and 43 sample pieces. Battery pack testing starts at USD 8,220 with a 6-8 week lead time. — [BatterySpace IEC 62133 page](https://www.batteryspace.com/iec62133.aspx)
- Chinese lab estimates for UN 38.3 are around USD 1,500-2,500 per model. — [JJR Lab](https://www.jjrlab.com/news/how-much-does-un383-testing-cost.html); [JJR Lab](https://www.jjrlab.com/news/what-is-a-un383-test-report-how-much-does-it-cost.html)
- A UN 38.3 test summary is required in the supply chain for shipping lithium cells and batteries. — [IATA Lithium Battery Guidance Document 2026](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf)

**Chargers and devices (CE: LVD/EMC/RoHS)**
- CE for chargers: about USD 4,000-15,000 (low end with pre-certified modules, high end for custom designs), with LVD, EMC and RoHS as the core directives, EN 62368-1 as the usual safety standard, and 5-12 weeks. *Source is a marketplace seller blog, so treat as indicative.* — [Alibaba seller blog, 2026](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/electronics-accessories/ce-certified-charger-compliance-guide-alibaba-b2b)
- Nemko (Norwegian, Oslo-based) tests to IEC/EN 62368-1 and offers EMC testing and CE attestations. — [Nemko IEC/EN 62368-1](https://www.nemko.com/product-testing/safety-testing/it-audio-video/hazard-based-standard-iec-en-62368-1-csa-ul-62368-1); [Nemko IT/AV certification](https://www.nemko.com/it-av-equipment-certification)

**RED cybersecurity / Cyber Resilience Act**
- Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 applies from 1 Aug 2025. It covers network protection, personal data/privacy and fraud protection for specified categories of radio equipment. — [SOS Electronic](https://www.soselectronic.com/en-ki/articles/sos-supplier-of-solution/the-delegated-act-on-cybersecurity-under-red-eu-202230-comes-into-effect-on-1-august-2025-3074); [DEKRA](https://www.dekra.us/en/digital-product-solutions/red-da-cybersecurity-europe/)
- The Commission launched a consultation on 10 Dec 2025 to repeal 2022/30 with effect from 11 Dec 2027, when the Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) applies in full. Norway is tracking the repeal in its EEA notes (June 2026). — [Eleos Compliance](https://www.eleoscompliance.com/en/article/european-union-repeal-red-cybersecurity-delegated-regulation-eu-202230); [Regjeringen EØS-notat: Oppheving av RED cybersikkerhetsforordning](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/sub/eos-notatbasen/notatene/2026/juni/oppheving-av-red-cybersikkerhetsforordning-202230/id3166994/)

**General Product Safety, RoHS, REACH, WEEE (primary texts)**
- GPSR (EU) 2023/988 has applied since 13 Dec 2024. It requires a responsible economic operator in the EU, traceability information, risk analysis and technical documentation, and accident notification. — [Regulation (EU) 2023/988, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj)
- RoHS (Directive 2011/65/EU) applies to chargers and devices as EEE. — [Directive 2011/65/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/65/oj)
- WEEE (Directive 2012/19/EU) EPR applies to chargers and devices. The Battery Regulation's Art. 11 cross-refers to EEE as defined in WEEE Art. 3(1)(a). — [Directive 2012/19/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2012/19/oj); [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 11](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- The Battery Regulation's Art. 6 restricts mercury, cadmium and lead in batteries and refers to REACH (Reg. 1907/2006) for other substances. — [Reg. 2023/1542](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)

### Inferences
- **Indicative compliance budget for launch** (excluding consultant fees; cell suppliers may already hold UN 38.3/IEC 62133-2 reports for the bare cell):
  - The UBS cell is a protected cell with an added NFC chip and fuel gauge, so it is a new assembly. Expect to re-test UN 38.3 and IEC 62133-2 on the final UBS cell even if the inner cell is certified: about USD 7-20k per model, 6-12 weeks, around 40-70 samples each. Three sizes, plus a LiFePO₄ variant if offered, gives roughly **USD 25-80k** for cells.
  - Charger: **USD 4-15k** per model (EN 62368-1, EMC, RoHS). If the charger reads NFC, add RED radio testing (EN 300 330 for 13.56 MHz, plus EN 301 489 EMC). Radio cost could not be sourced (see Gaps).
  - Each device class: similar CE costs. Devices with NFC readers fall under RED. If they have no internet or network connectivity, the 2022/30 network-protection requirement likely does not apply, but privacy and CRA analysis is needed.
  - Timeline: about 3-6 months from frozen design to full test reports, if samples are ready.
- **NFC in the cell:** it is unclear whether a passive NFC tag embedded in a battery makes the cell "radio equipment" under RED. It probably makes the cell a "product with digital elements" under the CRA if it carries data or firmware readable over a data connection. CRA vulnerability reporting obligations start 11 Sep 2026 and full obligations on 11 Dec 2027. **Ask a RED/CRA specialist (e.g. Nemko or a notified body) early.** This is the most novel compliance question in the UBS design.
- The Battery Regulation has no portable-battery safety article (Art. 12 safety covers stationary storage only), so cell safety is enforced via GPSR. IEC 62133-2 test reports are the practical evidence of GPSR compliance and are needed for marketplaces and retailers.

## 5. Product liability exposure and insurance

### Takeaway
Anodyne faces **strict (no-fault) liability** for defective products. The new Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853 must be transposed by **9 Dec 2026**. It brings software and firmware (relevant to the NFC/fuel-gauge firmware) explicitly within scope and widens the set of liable operators. Lithium cells sold loose to consumers are a high-severity fire risk category, and insurers are reported to be tightening cover around battery fires.

### Cited Findings
- Directive (EU) 2024/2853 replaces Directive 85/374/EEC. Member States must transpose it by 9 Dec 2026, and it applies to products placed on the market after that date. It keeps strict liability and explicitly includes software (operating systems, firmware, applications, AI systems) and digital manufacturing files as "products". Fulfilment service providers and, in some cases, distributors can be liable. — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/eu-introduces-comprehensive-digitalera-product-liability-directive); [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/portugal/art/new-directive-on-liability-for-defective-products)
- Waste operators face difficulty getting affordable insurance because of battery fires, and Member States have proposed a producer-financed compensation fund for fire damage. — [EUWID, 16 Dec 2025](https://www.euwid-recycling.com/news/policy/austria-germany-and-lithuania-press-european-commission-for-swift-decision-on-battery-deposit-scheme-151225/)

### Inferences
- Firmware in the cell (fuel gauge, NFC data) is a "product" under the new PLD. Defective state-of-charge reporting that leads to over-discharge or overcharge could create liability even if the cell chemistry itself is fine.
- Selling devices **without** cells moves part of the risk. If a user puts a non-UBS or counterfeit cell in an Anodyne device, liability depends on foreseeable misuse and warnings. Mechanical or electronic keying to genuine UBS cells (via NFC) would help defend against claims. However, Art. 11(8) prohibits *software* that impedes "compatible" batteries, so how authentication is used must be checked by a lawyer.
- Insurance: budget for product liability cover that explicitly includes lithium batteries and recall costs, and expect underwriters to ask for IEC 62133-2/UN 38.3 reports, QA (incoming cell inspection) and traceability. NFC IDs help with targeted recalls. **A broker with battery/e-mobility experience is needed.** No premium figures were found.

### Gaps
- Norwegian transposition status of the new PLD (EEA incorporation / changes to produktansvarsloven) was not found.
- No premium benchmarks found for small lithium-battery sellers in Norway/EU.

## 6. Transport and logistics constraints for shipping cells to consumers

### Takeaway
Shipping loose cells to consumers is the hardest operational constraint. **Posten (Norway Post) does not accept loose lithium cells.** It accepts lithium batteries only installed in equipment, at most 2 batteries or 4 cells. By air, UN3480 (cells alone) is **forbidden on passenger aircraft** and must be at **≤30% state of charge**. Since **1 Jan 2026**, UN3481 *packed with* equipment must also be at ≤30% SoC. By road, ADR special provision 188 gives relief for small cells (≤20 Wh per cell). Anodyne's "devices ship without cells" model is easy to post. Cells need a dangerous-goods-capable road courier or a negotiated business agreement.

### Cited Findings
- Posten: "Loose batteries cannot be sent." Lithium batteries/cells are accepted only when "installed in the equipment they are intended to be used in" and protected against damage and short-circuit. Content is "limited to two lithium batteries/four lithium cells". Damaged or defective batteries are never accepted. For more, or for non-installed batteries, contact customer service (22 03 00 00). Business customers are referred to bring.no. — [Posten: Dangerous and forbidden content](https://posten.no/en/sending/forbidden-content)
- IATA 2026: lithium-ion batteries packed by themselves (PI 965, UN3480) "must be shipped at a state of charge (SoC) not exceeding 30% of their rated capacity" and "cannot be shipped on a passenger aircraft unless approved by the relevant authorities". The Section IA package limit is 35 kg on cargo aircraft only, forbidden on passenger aircraft. — [IATA Lithium Battery Guidance Document 2026](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf)
- IATA 2026: from 1 Jan 2026, lithium-ion cells and batteries **packed with equipment** (PI 966) must be at ≤30% SoC (Section I). Section II cells and batteries above 2.7 Wh must also be at ≤30% SoC. Higher SoC needs state approval. — [IATA Lithium Battery Guidance Document 2026](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf); [Labelmaster/Lion: new SoC limit in effect 1 Jan 2026](https://www.lion.com/lion-news/december-2025/new-lithium-battery-state-of-charge-limit-in-effect-jan-1)
- A UN 38.3 test summary must be available for shipping. — [IATA guidance 2026](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf)
- By road (ADR), special provision 188 exempts small cells and batteries (Li-ion cells up to 20 Wh, batteries up to 100 Wh) from most dangerous-goods requirements if packages carry the lithium battery mark. Otherwise ADR-trained drivers and placarded vehicles are needed. *Taken from a search summary of Norwegian/Danish guidance (DSB, DEKRA). The DSB FAQ page returned 404.* — [DSB FAQ litiumbatterier](https://dsb.no/lover/farlige-stoffer/transport-av-farlig-gods/veiledning-og-informasjon/ofte-stilte-sporsmal---litiumbatterier/ExportWord); [DEKRA guide 08](https://dekra.dk/media/xaeb1omh/guide-08.pdf)
- Other postal operators have similar rules (e.g. PostNord special terms, PostNL lithium rules). — [PostNord special terms](https://www.postnord.no/siteassets/pdfs/special-terms-and-conditions-for-parcels.pdf); [PostNL](https://www.postnl.be/en/lithium/)

### Inferences
- Typical UBS cell energies: 14500 ≈ 2.5-3.7 Wh, 18650 ≈ 9-13 Wh, 21700 ≈ 15-19 Wh (at 3.6-3.7 V nominal). All are under the 20 Wh per-cell threshold, so SP 188 road relief and IATA Section II-style small-cell provisions are potentially available. Most are above 2.7 Wh, so the 30% SoC rule applies by air. This is the developer's inference; verify per cell model.
- **Practical model:** ship devices (no cells) by ordinary Posten/Bring parcel. Ship cells by road within Norway/Nordics using a carrier with a lithium/DG agreement (Bring business, PostNord, DHL Freight, DB Schenker), or by parcel lockers if the carrier allows SP 188 packages. Cross-border EU orders may need an EU fulfilment warehouse to avoid air transport.
- **The 30% SoC shipping rule fits the NFC fuel gauge:** each cell can prove its SoC at dispatch, which helps with compliance evidence.
- **Return logistics for the EUR 3 credit:** used and waste cells being returned are dangerous goods, and possibly waste under ADR SP 377. Consumers cannot simply post them back via Posten, since loose batteries are barred. Returns should go through retail drop-off points or PRO collection points, with NFC used to credit the account. Mail-back would need a specially packaged DG return kit, which is costly. This is a major design constraint for the return credit.

- Exact IATA PI 965 Section II per-package cell limits not extracted (PDF partially readable).
- ADR SP 377 (waste batteries) not verified from a primary source.

## 7. Devices sold without batteries, the common charger precedent, and EU interest in battery interoperability

### Takeaway
I found **no EU/EEA rule requiring a device to be sold with its battery**. The common charger rules show the EU is willing to mandate interoperability and to "unbundle" accessories so devices can be sold without them. Article 11 of the Battery Regulation says devices must accept "another compatible battery", and Art. 94(6) told the Commission to study harmonised chargers for batteries in EEE. That is the clearest signal of interest in battery-level interoperability. I found no Commission initiative on standard *battery formats* for consumer devices beyond these.

### Cited Findings
- Directive (EU) 2022/2380 (common charger, amending the RED) mandates USB-C charging for phones, tablets, cameras, headphones and similar devices from 28 Dec 2024, and for laptops from 28 Apr 2026. It also requires that consumers can buy such devices **without a charger** ("unbundling"), with a pictogram showing whether a charger is included. — [Directive (EU) 2022/2380, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2380/oj)
- Battery Regulation recital 41: "The interoperability of chargers for specific categories of batteries could reduce unnecessary waste and costs." Art. 94(6) requires a Commission assessment by 1 Jan 2025 of harmonised common-charger standards for LMT batteries and for rechargeable batteries in EEE. — [Reg. 2023/1542](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Art. 11(6) defines replaceability by reference to "another compatible battery", and Art. 11(8) prohibits software that impedes compatible batteries. — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 11](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- "Portable batteries of general use" are defined by interoperability ("specifically designed to be interoperable") and a closed list of common formats that does not include Li-ion cylindrical sizes. — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 3(10)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)
- Art. 11(1) obligations (removability, instructions, online information) fall on persons placing "products incorporating portable batteries" on the market. — [Reg. 2023/1542 Art. 11(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj)

### Inferences
- **Cell-less devices:** a device shipped without any battery arguably does not "incorporate" a portable battery when placed on the market. Art. 11's removability duty may therefore not apply formally, though the device is designed to meet its spirit. The device still needs CE (LVD/EMC/RoHS, plus RED if NFC), WEEE registration, and GPSR safety, including **clear instructions on which cells are compatible and how to insert them safely**. GPSR's information and warning duties are the practical "labelling of battery type compatibility" requirement. No specific EU rule mandating a compatibility label was found. Whether Art. 11(7) spare-part duties attach to Anodyne devices when the battery is sold separately is unclear. **Legal opinion needed.**
- Selling without batteries creates two practical risks: (1) consumers inserting unprotected or counterfeit 18650s, a liability and safety issue; (2) marketplaces and retailers treating "batteries not included" products as normal (no barrier found). Clear UBS marking, mechanical keying and an NFC check help, but the NFC check must not become a software lockout of "compatible" batteries under Art. 11(8). The legal question is whether a non-UBS cell counts as "compatible" for an Anodyne device.
- **Policy story:** the common charger directive shows the EU will legislate interoperability when market fragmentation creates waste (charger unbundling ≈ selling devices without cells). Art. 11 plus Art. 94(6) show the Commission is already thinking about battery replaceability and charger interoperability. UBS fits as a voluntary standard that could later become a harmonised standard (CEN/CENELEC) under the Battery Regulation or ESPR. That route needs standardisation-body engagement (e.g. Standard Norge / NEK in Norway).

### Gaps
- Did not find the Commission's Art. 94(6) assessment output, or any Commission statement specifically on standardising battery formats (beyond chargers) for consumer devices.
- Did not check the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 or product-specific ecodesign acts (e.g. smartphones/tablets battery durability rules) for battery-format interoperability provisions.
