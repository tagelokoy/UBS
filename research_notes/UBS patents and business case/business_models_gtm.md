# Business models, unit economics, funding and go-to-market for UBS and Anodyne

Scope note: research done 7 October 2026 with about 22 search/fetch calls. Prices come from distributor and supplier listings, which are noisy. Treat every number as an order-of-magnitude range, not a quote. Items I believe are true but did not verify in this session are listed under Gaps, marked "unverified".

## 1. Business model options and how comparable organizations make money (standard body, brand-then-open platform, component/licensing, premium brand); which order of moves has worked

### Takeaway
Standard bodies earn money from member dues, logo licences and per-product certification fees, at the level of thousands of USD per company per year. That only works once many companies already want the logo. Every battery platform that succeeded at sharing (Ryobi ONE+, Bosch Power for All, Gogoro) started as one strong company's proprietary system and opened it later, on that company's terms. The evidence points to "brand/platform first, open standard later". A lone founder has no record of starting with a standard body.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Standard bodies: fee levels**
- USB-IF: annual membership is US$5,000 and includes a vendor ID. A logo licence is US$3,500 for two years for non-members and free for members. A vendor ID bought on its own costs US$6,000. The logo may only be used on products that pass USB-IF compliance testing. — [USB-IF (usb.org/node/1809)](https://www.usb.org/node/1809); [USB-IF Logo Trademark License Agreement](https://usb.org/node/1811)
- Bluetooth SIG: the product declaration (qualification) fee for Adopter members was US$9,600, and Associate members pay US$4,800. One source says the Adopter fee rises to US$12,000 from 1 March 2026. Declaration fees apply to new, modified or rebranded Bluetooth products. — [Nordic DevZone: Bluetooth SIG Fees](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/126424/bluetooth-sig-fees/558576); [Bluetooth SIG payment terms](https://www.bluetooth.com/payment-terms/); [IB Lenhardt: Bluetooth SIG](https://ib-lenhardt.com/product-certification/bluetooth-sig)
- Wireless Power Consortium (Qi) was formed on 17 December 2008. Its founding is traced to Fulton Innovation, a subsidiary of Alticor (Amway's parent). The eight founding members were ConvenientPower, Fulton Innovation, Logitech, National Semiconductor, Philips, Sanyo, Shenzhen Sang Fei and Texas Instruments. — [Wikipedia: Wireless Power Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_Power_Consortium)

**(b) A brand creates a platform and opens it later**
- Ryobi ONE+ launched in 1996. Its 18V battery design has stayed compatible since then, and there are now more than 300 18V tools. TTI marked 25 years in about 2021 with more than 225 products. — [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/2207521/what-is-ryobi-one-plus-promise-which-tools-does-it-apply-to/); [Roofing Contractor (TTI press)](https://roofingcontractor.com/articles/95739-ryobi-18v-one-platform-celebrates-25-years-with-225-plus-products)
- Bosch and Husqvarna Group (through Gardena) founded Power for All in 2020. It is a licensing arrangement: Bosch produces the common 18V battery for all brands in the alliance. Member brands include Gardena, Bosch Home Appliances, Husqvarna, GLORIA, Wagner, Rapid, Steinel, Kübler Workwear and PerfectPro, with roughly 100 tools from 10 brands at the time of reporting. — [The Licensing Letter](https://www.thelicensingletter.com/?p=5523); [Gloria Garten](https://gloriagarten.de/en/home-and-garden/battery-world/18v-power-for-all-devices); [ToolGuyd](https://toolguyd.com/bosch-cordless-power-tool-battery-system-alliances/); [Bosch DIY](https://www.bosch-diy.com/ae/en/landing/power-for-all/power-for-all)
- Gogoro runs an open "Powered by Gogoro Network" programme. It gives vehicle OEMs the know-how to design scooters that use its battery swapping. It has eight vehicle partners, including Yamaha, Hero MotoCorp, Yadea, Dachangjiang, Tailing eReady (a joint venture with Suzuki), AeonMotor, PGO and CMC eMOVING. Yamaha's EC-05 uses Gogoro's platform. — [RideApart](https://www.rideapart.com/news/361449/gogoro-network-battery-swapping-standards-push/); [Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/4345783); [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2021/09/16/next-stop-nasdaq-ev-battery-swapping-network-gogoro-is-going-public/)
- Gogoro passed one million swappable batteries in 2022. — [CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2022/02/24/gogoro-goes-big-with-one-millionth-swappable-battery/)
- I found no public figures for what Gogoro earns from partner licences. — (search returned none; see Gaps)

**(c) Ecosystem and component model (Framework as the closest analogue)**
- Framework published documentation, CAD templates and reference designs for its Expansion Cards under open-source licences on GitHub. It then opened its Marketplace to third-party and community modules. — [Framework blog: Expansion Card Developer Program](https://frame.work/blog/expansion-card-developer-program--canada-launch); [Framework blog: Announcing the Framework Marketplace](https://frame.work/blog/announcing-the-framework-marketplace)
- Framework set aside US$100,000 of grants, discounts and hardware for one year to start the expansion-card ecosystem, and helped fund mass production of promising designs. — [Framework blog](https://frame.work/blog/expansion-card-developer-program--canada-launch)
- Framework raised a US$17M Series A-1 plus a US$1M community round from 100 investors at US$10,000 each. — [iTWire](https://itwire.com/business-it-news/hardware-and-storage/consumer-electronics-firm-framework-raises-us$17m-for-expansion)

**(d) Premium or mission-driven consumer brands**
- Teenage Engineering (Stockholm, about 90 staff): turnover was about EUR 17.5M in 2022, EUR 25.3M in 2023 and EUR 37.9M in 2024. Earnings before tax were about EUR 4.2M in 2024. — [Largest Companies](https://largestcompanies.com/company/Teenage-Engineering-AB-502717/closing-figures-and-key-ratios); [Syna](https://upplysningar.syna.se/foretag/5567183719/teenage-engineering-ab)
- Fairphone's revenue grew from EUR 10.8M (2018) to a peak of EUR 58.9M (2022). EBITDA was −EUR 14.3M in 2023 and +EUR 1.7M in 2024, on 103,053 units sold in 2024. In 2023, impact investors (Invest-NL, ABN AMRO Sustainable Impact Fund, Quadia) put in EUR 49M. — [Wikipedia: Fairphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairphone)
- Fairphone reports that its smartphone shipments grew 42% in 2025 while the overall market shrank. — [Fairphone press release (PDF)](https://www.fairphone.com/press/pdf/Fairphone%27s%20Smartphone%20Shipments%20Surged%20by%2042%25%20in%202025%20-%20Despite%20Shrinking%20Global%20Smartphone%20Market.pdf)

### Inferences
- Standard-body income comes from many companies paying a few thousand USD each. That only works once the logo already pulls in customers. A UBS foundation started by one person would have close to zero fee income for years. Keep UBS as a free, published spec with a trademarked logo, and add a foundation only if adopters ask for one.
- The battery examples (Ryobi, Bosch, Gogoro) all follow the same order. One company ships a working, proprietary, compatible system and builds an installed base. It then invites partners who want access to that base. Bosch and Gogoro opened only after they had scale. This supports "Anodyne first, UBS opened in stages". Publishing the spec early costs little and helps credibility, but adoption will follow products, not documents.
- Model (c), selling a "smart cap" NFC/gauge module or a reference bay design, is the most capital-light route to revenue. Its buyers are B2B and hard to reach without a track record, and it competes with commodity parts from TI, ST and NXP.
- Model (d) is possible but slow. Teenage Engineering took about 15 years to reach about EUR 38M turnover. Fairphone needed tens of millions of euros of impact capital and was loss-making in some years. A premium brand needs design talent, capital and channel access, which a part-time founder does not have.
- Framework's US$100k developer fund is a small, copyable tactic: fund third parties to build the first compatible devices.

### Gaps
- No public data on Gogoro or Bosch Power for All licence fees or royalty structure.
- WPC and CSA membership fee tiers were not retrieved. Unverified: both use tiered annual membership plus certification fees.
- Unverified, from background knowledge: USB 1.0 (1996) came from a consortium of Compaq, DEC, IBM, Intel, Microsoft, NEC and Nortel, so it is not a model for a solo founder. Prusa grew from one person's open-source RepRap work into a profitable company that sells its own hardware, which again shows the open design paid off through product sales, not licences. Neither was checked this session.

## 2. Chicken-and-egg adoption: how platforms got the first devices and cells into the market; which partners matter most

### Takeaway
Successful platforms solved chicken-and-egg by being both sides at once at first. Ryobi made both tools and batteries. Bosch made the battery for every alliance brand. Gogoro ran the swap network and sold its own scooters before partners joined. For UBS, Anodyne would have to be the first cell supplier and the first device maker. Partners come only when there is an installed base or a regulatory reason.

### Cited Findings
- In Power for All, one maker (Bosch) produces the common battery for all partner brands. Partners bring tools, not cells. — [The Licensing Letter](https://www.thelicensingletter.com/?p=5523); [Gloria Garten](https://gloriagarten.de/en/home-and-garden/battery-world/18v-power-for-all-devices)
- Gogoro's partners got shorter R&D times and access to the existing GoStation network. Yamaha used the platform to speed up its own e-scooter development. — [RideApart](https://www.rideapart.com/news/361449/gogoro-network-battery-swapping-standards-push/)
- Flashlight brands already sell their own "smart" proprietary cells. The Nitecore NL2150HPR (21700, 5000 mAh) and the Fenix ARB-L21-5000U and ARB-L21-6000B have built-in USB-C charging and protection. This means the flashlight segment already sells added-value branded cells, but each brand's are proprietary. — [Battery Junction: Nitecore NL2150HPR](https://www.batteryjunction.com/nitecore-nl2150hpr); [Fenix Store: ARB-L21-5000U](https://fenix-store.com/products/fenix-21700-rechargeable-battery-5000u); [Focus Nordic: Fenix ARB-L21-6000B](https://www.focusnordic.com/products/batteries-power-supply/batteries/photo-lithium-batteries/fenix-battery-arb-l21-6000b-6000mah-21700-battery)
- Regulatory tailwind: EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542 Article 11 requires that portable batteries in products placed on the EU market from 18 February 2027 be removable and replaceable by the end user with commercially available tools. Commission guidance says the obligation applies to the whole battery, not individual cells. — [EUR-Lex C/2025/214 (Commission guidelines)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2025/214/oj/eng); [Dudkowiak](https://www.dudkowiak.com/blog/battery-replacement-and-removability-starting-in-2027-new-guidelines-from-the-european-commission/); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/avoiding-the-redesign-the-eus-latest-position-on-battery-removability); [Reach24h: exemptions](https://en.reach24h.com/news/industry-news/sustainability/eu-portable-battery-removability-exemptions-2026)
- Molicel (P28A, P30B) and EVE (25P) 18650 cells are sold through Western distributors in small quantities, so prototypes can use genuine name-brand cells without a factory MOQ. — [18650 Battery Store: Molicel](https://www.18650batterystore.com/collections/vendors?q=molicel); [Fogstar: EVE 25P](https://www.fogstar.co.uk/products/eve-25p)

### Inferences
- Partner priority:
  1. One cell pack-assembler or protected-cell OEM in China willing to build the UBS cap (protection, NFC, gauge) onto bought-in Molicel, EVE, BAK or Lishen cells. Tier-1 cell makers (Samsung SDI, Murata, Molicel) will not engage with an idea-stage brand. Pack houses and protected-cell assemblers will, for money.
  2. One or two enthusiast flashlight brands (Wurkkos, Sofirn and similar are more reachable than Nitecore or Fenix). They already use 18650/21700 cells and their buyers handle loose cells. Nitecore and Fenix earn money on proprietary USB-C cells, so they have a reason to resist.
  3. Nordic retail (Clas Ohlson, Jula, Biltema, Elkjøp) only after there is a product with sell-through data.
- The Article 11 deadline gives device makers a reason to design around removable cells now. It could support a pitch to small device brands ("comply with Article 11 using an open cell standard"). But the rule applies to batteries as a whole and has exemptions, so it does not force anyone to adopt standard cells.
- The power-tool approach (closed 18V packs) is not under threat from UBS. Tool makers are the hardest partners to win and should be left until last.

### Gaps
- No primary-source interviews with Molicel, EVE, Samsung SDI, Murata, BAK or Lishen about appetite for a third-party "smart cell" standard.
- Nordic retailer listing terms and margins (Clas Ohlson, Jula, Biltema, Elkjøp) were not found.
- Unverified: the EU battery passport (Article 77) applies from February 2027 only to LMT, industrial >2 kWh and EV batteries, not portable cells. So UBS's NFC "health passport" would be voluntary and not a compliance tool. Not checked this session.

## 3. Unit economics: cell cost, NFC and gauge adder, markups, ODM/OEM options, MOQs and tooling

### Takeaway
A bare name-brand 18650 cell costs about US$3–4.5 at distributor volume pricing. Factory-direct prices are lower but were not verifiable. Adding NFC (about US$0.3–2) and a fuel gauge (about US$1.5–2), plus a PCB, cap and assembly, likely brings a UBS cell's cost to roughly US$6–10 at low volume. That compares with Fenix and Nitecore USB-C cells retailing around US$20–35 (unverified). Safety certification costs several thousand USD per model. Generic Shenzhen ODM power banks start at MOQ 1,000.

### Cited Findings
**Cells**
- Molicel P28A 18650 is listed from about US$3.35 per cell at volume and P30B from about US$3.60. Distributor MOQs are tiny (2–4 pieces). These are distributor wholesale tiers, not factory OEM prices. — [18650 Battery Store wholesale](https://www.18650batterystore.com/pages/wholesale-18650-batteries); [18650 Battery Store: Molicel](https://www.18650batterystore.com/collections/vendors?q=molicel)
- One aggregator puts genuine Molicel P28A at US$3.35–4.23 per cell. — [Accio](https://www.accio.com/business/18650-li-ion) (aggregator, lower confidence)

**NFC and fuel-gauge ICs (distributor prices, 1k-unit tier)**
- ST25DV04K: about US$1.98 at 1,000–1,990 units from one distributor. Other listings for variants show about US$0.79 and US$0.29 at 1,000+. The spread reflects different variants and reels. — [Findchips ST25DV04KC](https://origin-www.findchips.com/search/st25dv04kc-ie8t3); [element14 TW](https://tw.element14.com/en-TW/stmicroelectronics/st25dv04k-jfr8d3/rfid-read-write-13-567mhz-ufdfpn/dp/3364972RL); [LCSC C155601](https://www.lcsc.com/product-image/C155601.html)
- NXP NTAG I2C plus (NT3H2111/2211): about £0.54, CHF 0.59 and S$0.94 at 1,000+ (roughly US$0.65–0.70). The suggested resale price is from US$0.29 at 500+. — [Farnell UK](https://uk.farnell.com/nxp/nt3h2111w0ft1/rfid-read-write-13-56mhz-soic/dp/2663149); [Farnell CH](https://ch.farnell.com/nxp/nt3h2111w0fttj/rfid-lesen-schreiben-13-56mhz/dp/2663150); [element14 SG](https://sg.element14.com/nxp/nt3h2211w0ft1x/rfid-read-write-13-56mhz-soic/dp/2663152); [NXP](https://www.nxp.com/company/about-nxp/newsroom/NW-EXTEND-NTAG-I2C)
- TI BQ27441-G1 fuel gauge: about EUR 1.65 at 1,000+ (the search summary showed "€1,650" with a European decimal comma) and about £1.59 at 250+. — [Farnell AT](https://at.farnell.com/texas-instruments/bq27441drzt-g1a/battery-fuel-gauge-li-ion-4-5v/dp/3008752); [RS UK](https://uk.rs-online.com/web/p/battery-management/1628500); [DigiKey](https://digikey.cn/en/products/detail/texas-instruments/BQ27441DRZT-G1A/4733020)

**Certification (safety and transport)**
- UN38.3 transport test: US$1,500 per model at one Chinese lab, including the samples. Another source gives US$5,000–7,000 and 4–6 weeks. The altitude simulation test is cited as causing about 40% of first-time failures. — [aaportablepower](https://aaportablepower.com/UN383test.html); [Tritek](https://tritekbattery.com/un38-3-certified-battery-pack-what-it-means-and-how-to-source/)
- IEC 62133 combined cell and pack: US$6,350 per model at one lab. Another source gives US$6,000–10,000 and 6–8 weeks for IEC/UL. — [aaportablepower](https://aaportablepower.com/UN383test.html); [Tritek](https://tritekbattery.com/un38-3-certified-battery-pack-what-it-means-and-how-to-source/); [Epec](https://www.epectec.com/batteries/battery-pack-certifications.html)

**ODM/OEM**
- Shenzhen power-bank ODMs list MOQ 1,000 pieces. Unit prices run about US$7–8 for basic 10,000 mAh PD models and US$22–24 for 20,000 mAh PD+QC models. Several have in-house injection moulding and launch new private moulds every month. — [Alibaba price comparison](https://www.alibaba.com/price-comparison/amazon-com-syncbank); [Global Sources: ITC](https://exhibitions.globalsources.com/exhibitors/HK/shenzhen-itc-technology-co-ltd_2008835882666/); [Global Sources: E-Teck](https://e-teckoffer.manufacturer.globalsources.com/homepage_6003002395014.htm)

### Inferences
- Rough per-cell cost of a UBS 18650 at 1–5k units (my estimate): bare cell US$3–4.5, protection PCM about US$0.3–0.8, NFC US$0.3–2, gauge US$1.5–2, cap PCB, housing, label and assembly US$1–3. Total about US$6–12. Gauge and NFC at 1k pricing add about 40–100% to the bare-cell cost. This is a major issue for the spec: a mandatory gauge in every cell is expensive, and a passive NFC tag with data written by the charger would be much cheaper. At 100k+ volumes, factory prices could plausibly halve the adder, but that is unverified.
- With a typical consumer-electronics retail price of 2.5–4× landed cost (unverified, see Gaps), a UBS 18650 would retail at roughly NOK 150–350. That is the same as or above Fenix and Nitecore USB-C cells, which already have a premium position.
- Fixed costs per cell size, before the first sale: UN38.3 plus IEC 62133 is about US$8–17k per model. Three sizes (14500, 18650, 21700) mean about US$25–50k in tests alone, plus re-tests after failures. Each device and charger needs its own EMC, CE and safety work.
- The cheapest product to bring to market first is a charger or a device without cells, sold with a "use any protected 18650/21700 today" message. It avoids cell certification and lets Anodyne test demand before it owns cell liability.

### Gaps
- Factory-direct (Alibaba/OEM) prices and MOQs for protected or custom-cap 18650/21700 cells were not retrieved. Unverified: typical OEM MOQs are 1k–10k cells.
- Injection-mould tooling costs for a custom enclosure were not found. Unverified rule of thumb: roughly US$3k–30k per mould depending on complexity.
- Typical retail markups and Nordic retailer margins were not verified.
- Retail prices of Fenix and Nitecore USB-C cells were not captured (the product pages were found but prices not extracted).

## 4. Funding paths in Norway/EU at idea stage

### Takeaway
Realistic early money in Norway is small and non-dilutive. Innovasjon Norge's market-clarification or start-up grant (up to about NOK 100–150k, up to 100%) is the obvious first step, followed by commercialisation grants (up to NOK 750k at 75%). SkatteFUNN gives a 19% tax deduction on approved R&D but needs an AS with real R&D costs. The EIC Accelerator (grant up to EUR 2.5M) is for TRL 6–8 companies with a team and is out of reach for an idea-stage solo founder.

### Cited Findings
- Innovasjon Norge market clarification grant (markedsavklaringstilskudd): up to 100% coverage, maximum NOK 150,000. An older source says NOK 100,000. Start-up grant 1 (oppstartstilskudd 1) is for companies under 3 years old, up to 100%, maximum NOK 150,000. Commercialisation grant phase 1 and phase 2: up to 75% coverage, maximum NOK 750,000. A start-up loan (oppstartslån) also exists. — [PNO Innovation](https://www.pnoinnovation.com/no/finansiering/oppstartstilskudd/); [StartupLab: Funding through Innovation Norway](https://startuplabno.notion.site/Funding-Through-Innovation-Norway-Office-Hours-1-8fc8ad0526c348579c0dd3ea6a34d586). Innovasjon Norge's own service page returned 404 at the URL I tried, so the current 2026 amounts are not confirmed from the primary source.
- Innovasjon Norge's 2026 assignment letters from the government exist and set the year's framework. — [Regjeringen: oppdragsbrev 2026](https://www.regjeringen.no/contentassets/036ffda0c81e4b3f99456a78ec31df0e/innovasjon-norge-in-oppdragsbrev-for-2026.pdf)
- SkatteFUNN: a tax deduction of 19% of R&D project costs, with costs capped at NOK 25 million per income year. There is a consultation on changes for 2026/2027, and SMEs complain that auditor costs are disproportionate for small projects. — [Forskningsrådet: SkatteFUNN 2026 call](https://www.forskningsradet.no/utlysninger/2026/skattefunn-skattefradrag-for-forskning-og-utvikling-i-et-nyskapende-naringsliv/); [Regjeringen: høringsnotat SkatteFUNN 2026](https://www.regjeringen.no/contentassets/c22a1785cc094644aead80ebe0853151/horingsnotat-endringer-i-skattefunn-2026.pdf); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/no/nb/innsikt/skatt-regulatorisk/skattefunn-konsernrammer-og-nye-krav-2027.html)
- EIC Accelerator: grant up to EUR 2.5M for TRL 6–8, plus equity of EUR 1–10M as blended finance. Eligible applicants are SMEs, start-ups, and individuals intending to found one in an EU member state or associated country (Norway is associated with Horizon Europe). The 2026 cut-offs are 7 Jan, 4 Mar, 6 May, 8 Jul, 2 Sep and 4 Nov. The process has three steps (short proposal, full proposal, jury interview), and the 2026 budget is about EUR 634M. — [Innovation Israel: EIC Accelerator](https://innovationisrael.org.il/iserd/eic-accelerator/); [USI](https://www.usi.ch/en/feeds/33615); [eDotto](https://www.edotto.com/articolo/eic-accelerator-2026-finanziamenti-fino-a-10-milioni-per-startup-e-pmi-innovative) (secondary sources; the EIC work programme itself was not fetched)

**Crowdfunding as validation (included here because it is a funding path)**
- Of crowdfunded hardware campaigns, only 9% delivered on time, 33% failed to deliver at all, and 58% were delayed or changed. In another sample of 50 highly funded projects, 84% missed their ship date, with a median delay of two months. Projects funded at 10× their goal were half as likely to deliver at a given time as those funded at goal. Causes include manufacturing problems, shipping, scale and scope changes, and unexpected certification issues. — [Chalmers thesis "Hardware is Hard"](https://odr.chalmers.se/handle/20.500.12380/301564); [HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/most-heavilyfunded-kickstarter-projects-miss-their-ship-dates-cant-handle-success); [DSHR blog](https://blog.dshr.org/2017/10/crowdfunding.html) (several secondary summaries; the underlying studies are 2012–2017 data)

### Inferences
- Sequence: register an AS (needed for most Innovasjon Norge grants and SkatteFUNN). Apply for the market clarification grant (up to NOK 150k) to pay for customer interviews, partner talks and a prototype. Use SkatteFUNN if there is real R&D spend, such as cap/firmware development or cell testing. Only after paying customers or LOIs exist, apply for the commercialisation grant (up to NOK 750k at 75%). EIC is relevant only years later, if at all.
- Total realistic non-dilutive money in the first 1–2 years is roughly NOK 150k–1M. That is enough for validation and prototypes, not for a cell product line with three certified sizes.
- Crowdfunding works as a demand test but carries a high delivery risk for batteries, which are hazardous goods. It should fund a device or charger first, not cells.

### Gaps
- Innovasjon Norge's own 2026 pages were not reached (404). Confirm the amounts and the age and AS requirements directly.
- Nordic Innovation, Horizon Europe Cluster 5 battery calls and Battery 2030+ were not researched due to the tool budget. Unverified: Horizon calls generally need consortia and research partners and are not suitable for a solo founder.
- Forskningsrådet's "innovasjonsprosjekt i næringslivet" was not researched.

## 5. Cheap validation steps before spending real money

### Takeaway
The cheapest useful evidence comes from talking to 10–20 device makers and 2–3 Chinese protected-cell or pack assemblers, plus a waitlist on the existing site, before building anything. A working prototype can be made from distributor-bought Molicel/EVE cells and off-the-shelf NFC ICs that cost under US$1–2 each.

### Cited Findings
- Genuine name-brand cells are available in small quantities, from 2–4 pieces. — [18650 Battery Store wholesale](https://www.18650batterystore.com/pages/wholesale-18650-batteries); [Fogstar: EVE 25P](https://www.fogstar.co.uk/products/eve-25p)
- NFC dual-interface ICs with I²C (NTAG I2C plus, ST25DV) are stocked by Farnell, element14 and LCSC for under US$1–2 in single or low quantities. — [Farnell UK](https://uk.farnell.com/nxp/nt3h2111w0ft1/rfid-read-write-13-56mhz-soic/dp/2663149); [LCSC](https://www.lcsc.com/product-image/C155601.html)
- Framework's route was to publish CAD and reference designs openly and seed third-party developers with small grants. — [Framework blog](https://frame.work/blog/expansion-card-developer-program--canada-launch)
- The Innovasjon Norge market clarification grant exists to pay for exactly this "is there a market" phase, at up to 100%. — [PNO Innovation](https://www.pnoinnovation.com/no/finansiering/oppstartstilskudd/)

### Inferences
Staged plan, built around limited free time:
- **Stage 0 (0–3 months, under NOK 10k):**
  - Publish the UBS spec as a versioned draft (GitHub or PDF) with an open comment process.
  - Add a waitlist and a short survey to the existing site.
  - Contact about 20 small device brands (flashlight, outdoor and maker) and 3–5 Chinese protected-cell or pack assemblers. Record in writing whether they would build or buy, and at what price.
  - Kill criteria: fewer than 2 device makers interested, or no cell assembler willing to quote.
- **Stage 1 (3–9 months, about NOK 50–150k, ideally grant-funded):**
  - Make 10–20 prototype "smart caps" (protection, NFC, optional gauge) on bought-in cells.
  - Build one bay or charger prototype and get user tests in Norway.
  - Apply for the market clarification grant.
- **Stage 2 (9–24 months):**
  - Launch the first product that needs no cell certification: charger, flashlight or modular power-bank shell "for any protected 18650/21700".
  - Run a small crowdfunding or pre-order campaign with an ODM at MOQ around 1,000.
  - Only then certify one cell size (UN38.3 plus IEC 62133, about US$8–17k).
- **Stage 3:** only with sell-through data and partners, add more cell sizes, retail listings and a formal UBS foundation.

### Gaps
- No data found on conversion from waitlist to pre-order for hardware.
- No examples found of hardware design competitions used to kick-start an open standard.

## 6. Key risks: safety and liability, copying, cell makers, consumer willingness, NFC cost and space, fragmentation, founder time

### Takeaway
The biggest risks are liability for lithium cells (fire, transport and certification failures), the cost of the NFC and gauge adder per cell, and the founder's time. Big brands copying the idea is less of a threat than big brands ignoring it. Proprietary USB-C cells from Fenix and Nitecore already meet the "smart cell" need inside each brand.

### Cited Findings
- UN38.3 has a high first-time failure rate (the altitude test is cited at about 40%). Each failure means re-tests and delays. — [Tritek](https://tritekbattery.com/un38-3-certified-battery-pack-what-it-means-and-how-to-source/)
- Unexpected certification issues are a named cause of delays in crowdfunded hardware. — [Chalmers "Hardware is Hard"](https://odr.chalmers.se/handle/20.500.12380/301564)
- Competing incumbents already sell smart 21700 cells with protection and USB-C (Nitecore NL2150HPR, Fenix ARB-L21-5000U). — [Battery Junction](https://www.batteryjunction.com/nitecore-nl2150hpr); [Fenix Store](https://fenix-store.com/products/fenix-21700-rechargeable-battery-5000u)
- The EU removability rule (from 18 Feb 2027) has exemptions and applies to whole batteries. Brands can comply with proprietary packs and do not need standard cells. — [EUR-Lex C/2025/214](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2025/214/oj/eng); [Reach24h](https://en.reach24h.com/news/industry-news/sustainability/eu-portable-battery-removability-exemptions-2026)
- Fuel-gauge and NFC ICs together cost about US$2–4 at 1k distributor prices, a large fraction of a US$3–4.5 cell. — see the sources in section 3

### Inferences
- **Safety and liability:** as the brand owner placing cells on the EU/EEA market, Anodyne would be the producer under product-safety and battery rules. A single fire incident could end a small company. Product liability insurance and an AS (limited liability) are needed before selling any cell. Selling devices without cells first reduces this exposure but does not remove it.
- **NFC and gauge cost and space:** a 14500 cell has very little room for a cap PCB and antenna. A mandatory gauge raises cost by roughly 40–100% per cell at low volume. Make the gauge optional or put it in the device or charger, and keep NFC to a passive tag.
- **Copying:** an open, royalty-free spec cannot be "stolen", but a big brand could ship a compatible or near-compatible proprietary variant. The defence is the trademarked logo plus a certification programme, as USB-IF ties logo use to compliance testing.
- **Fragmentation:** existing proprietary smart cells (Fenix, Nitecore) mean the market is already fragmented. UBS becomes "one more format" unless it is backward-compatible with plain protected 18650/21700, which the current concept appears to be.
- **Founder time:** standards work (spec, comments, partner meetings) and brand work (ID, ODM, certification, logistics) are each a full-time job. Stage 0–1 is realistic part-time. Stage 2 is not, without a co-founder or a hardware-experienced partner.

### Gaps
- No data on consumer willingness to manage loose lithium cells (surveys or market share of loose-cell flashlights versus built-in battery products).
- Product liability insurance cost for a small Norwegian lithium-cell brand was not found.
- No reported incidents or recalls involving third-party smart cells were researched.

## 7. Case studies of founders who started open hardware standards or platforms as individuals or small teams

### Takeaway
Among the verified cases, none started as a lone founder publishing a standard. Qi and USB began as consortia of large companies. Framework, Fairphone and Teenage Engineering are product companies that opened parts of their ecosystem after shipping products and raising money. The realistic analogue for Anodyne is Framework: ship your own product, then publish module specs and fund third parties.

### Cited Findings
- Qi came from a corporate subsidiary (Fulton Innovation, part of Alticor/Amway) with seven other founding companies, including Philips, TI, Sanyo and Logitech. — [Wikipedia: WPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_Power_Consortium)
- Framework opened its expansion-card spec after launching its laptop, seeded the ecosystem with US$100k, and raised US$17M plus a US$1M community round. — [Framework blog](https://frame.work/blog/expansion-card-developer-program--canada-launch); [iTWire](https://itwire.com/business-it-news/hardware-and-storage/consumer-electronics-firm-framework-raises-us$17m-for-expansion)
- Fairphone needed EUR 49M of impact investment (2023), sold about 103k units in 2024 and only turned EBITDA-positive in 2024. — [Wikipedia: Fairphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairphone)
- Teenage Engineering is a profitable Swedish design-led hardware brand (about EUR 38M turnover and EUR 4M EBT in 2024, about 90 staff). — [Largest Companies](https://largestcompanies.com/company/Teenage-Engineering-AB-502717/closing-figures-and-key-ratios)
- Gogoro went public through a SPAC (2021–22) after building its own scooters and swap network, and only then signed big OEMs. — [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2021/09/16/next-stop-nasdaq-ev-battery-swapping-network-gogoro-is-going-public/)

### Inferences
- The pattern across cases: product first, money second, opening third. A solo founder's best lever is a well-written open spec plus one excellent first product. Licence revenue is not a realistic lever.
- The founder could also aim to have UBS adopted by an existing player, for example offering the spec to an enthusiast flashlight brand or a Nordic retailer's own brand, rather than building Anodyne in full. This lowers capital needs and liability but gives up control.

### Gaps
- Not researched this session (tool budget): Prusa's history, Teenage Engineering's "open-ish" ecosystem (OP-Z/Field accessories, 3D-printable parts), Nothing's funding and economics, and Anker's private-label origins. Unverified: Anker began as a third-party Amazon laptop-battery seller (about 2011) and grew through Amazon. Nothing raised large VC rounds. Both suggest a premium brand at scale needs serious capital.
- No documented case of an individual successfully creating a widely adopted consumer battery standard was found.
