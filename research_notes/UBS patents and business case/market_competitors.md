# Market and competitive landscape for UBS / Anodyne

Scope: global and Europe/EEA, current as of October 2026. Reliability labels used below:
**[SOLID]** = company report, regulator, official survey, or a primary retailer listing.
**[ROUGH]** = paid market-research summaries seen only through press releases or reseller pages, where the methodology could not be checked. Estimates from these firms often differ by 2-4x for the same market.
**[WEAK]** = aggregator, SEO, or "SWOT template" sites. Treat as indicative only.

## 1. Market size and growth

### Takeaway
All the adjacent markets are large: cordless power tools at roughly USD 13-14 bn, power banks at roughly USD 12-15 bn, consumer batteries at roughly USD 30 bn, and cylindrical Li-ion cells at somewhere between USD 9 bn and 23 bn, depending on the analyst. None of these figures measures the niche UBS actually targets: loose, user-replaceable, protected consumer cells sold together with cell-agnostic devices. That niche is a small fraction of these totals, and nobody publishes a figure for it. The analyst numbers vary a lot and should be quoted as ranges, not point values.

### Cited Findings
**Cordless power tools**
- [ROUGH] Grand View Research: global cordless power tools market USD 13.44 bn in 2024, projected USD 31.82 bn by 2033, CAGR 10.4% (2025-2033). Asia Pacific held the largest share, 33.4% in 2024. — [Grand View Research press release](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/press-release/global-cordless-power-tools-market)
- [SOLID] TTI (Milwaukee, Ryobi, AEG, Hoover/Vax) reported record 2025 sales of USD 15.3 bn, +4.4% reported and +4.1% in local currency. Milwaukee grew 7.9% in local currency, or 10.3% underlying after adjusting for tariff-related suspensions. Ryobi grew 5.4%, its second year of mid-single-digit growth after the 2022-23 post-pandemic dip. — [TTI 2025 results release](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/Techtronic+Industries+Delivers+Solid+Performance+in+2025/26097305.html); [TTI Annual Report 2025](https://www.ttigroup.com/documents/annual-report-2025/)
- Note: TTI's revenue alone (USD 15.3 bn, tools plus floorcare) is larger than Grand View's whole "cordless power tools" market (USD 13.4 bn). This shows how narrowly some analyst definitions are drawn. [Inference from the two sources above]
- [SOLID] Einhell Germany AG: 2025 revenue EUR 1,157.7 m (+4.3%). The Power X-Change battery platform accounted for **54% of group revenue**. — [Einhell corporate news, 21 Apr 2026](https://www.einhell.com/investor-relations/financial-news/corporate-news/detail/EQS-News_188704911_en/)

- [ROUGH] Technavio: consumer battery market to grow by USD 5.12 bn between 2023 and 2028. — [GlobeNewswire / Technavio](https://www.globenewswire.com/fr/news-release/2024/01/05/2804729/0/en/Consumer-Battery-Market-size-is-set-to-increase-by-USD-5-12-billion-between-2023-2028-The-growing-penetration-of-consumer-electronics-to-drive-the-market-growth-Technavio.html)

**Cylindrical Li-ion cells (18650/21700)**

These sources are reseller pages (GII, UnivDatos, 360ResearchReports), and the spread is very large. — [GII / Infiniti](https://giikorea.co.kr/report/infi1645434-global-cylindrical-lithium-ion-battery-market.html); [UnivDatos](https://univdatos.com/reports/cylindrical-lithium-ion-battery-market); [360ResearchReports](https://www.360researchreports.com/market-reports/cylindrical-lithium-ion-battery-market-201745)

**Small battery-powered devices**
- [ROUGH] Power banks: 2024 estimates range from USD 12.4 bn to USD 14.7 bn (Mordor Intelligence ~13.6 bn; others 14.0 and 14.7 bn). — [GMI](https://gminsights.com/industry-analysis/power-bank-market/market-size); [Verified Market Research](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/product/power-bank-market/); [GII/Mordor](https://www.gii.co.jp/report/moi922610-power-bank-market-growth-trends-forecast.html)
- [WEAK] Flashlights: 2024 estimates range wildly, from about USD 1.8 bn to 7.5 bn (SkyQuest: USD 1.73 bn in 2022 → 2.97 bn by 2031). A 4x spread shows the definitions are not comparable. — [GII / SkyQuest](https://www.gii.tw/report/sky1452749-global-flashlight-market-size-share-growth.html); [Emergen Research](https://www.emergenresearch.com/ar/industry-report/flashlight-market)

### Inferences
- The realistic addressable market for an Anodyne-style brand is the enthusiast and prosumer slice of flashlights, power banks, and loose cells, plus people frustrated with tool-battery lock-in. That is likely a few hundred million USD globally, not tens of billions. This is my estimate and is not sourced.
- Cordless tools are where "one battery, many devices" has already proved it can sell at scale: 54% of Einhell's revenue, and Ryobi growing on a single platform. But that demand is currently met by proprietary 18 V packs, not by loose cells.

### Gaps
- No credible figure exists for "loose retail 18650/21700 cells sold to consumers" or for the European portion of it.
- Could not get reputable figures for portable radios, electric toothbrushes, cordless vacuums, or game controllers within the research budget. BloombergNEF and Statista figures were behind paywalls or not found.
- No Europe-specific split for power tools or power banks from a source with a published methodology.

## 2. Existing "one battery, many devices" ecosystems

### Takeaway
Shared-battery platforms are a proven, mass-market concept. Each of the biggest has 300 or more tools, and the cross-brand alliances (CAS, Power for All, Einhell PXC) have grown fast in Europe. They are all built on proprietary 18 V packs inside tool categories. None covers small consumer electronics (radios, lights, power banks) at loose-cell scale, and none is an open standard. That is UBS's opening, but these incumbents are also its most formidable competitors.

### Cited Findings
- [SOLID] **Ryobi 18V ONE+** (TTI): Ryobi product pages say "over 300 products … work with one battery". — [Ryobi ONE+ product page](https://ryobitools.com/products/33287195084). Ryobi marked "25 years of battery compatibility with 225-plus products" (published around 2021), which points to a launch around 1996. — [Roofing Contractor](https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/95739-ryobi-18v-one-platform-celebrates-25-years-with-225-plus-products?v=preview)
- [SOLID] **Bosch Power for All Alliance** (founded 2020 by Bosch and Husqvarna/Gardena): an 18 V battery system made by Bosch, compatible since 2007. In June 2022 it had 10 brands (Bosch, Gardena, Gloria, Wagner, Rapid, Flymo, Steinel, plus new members Husqvarna, Kübler Workwear, PerfectPro). Bosch expected **more than 30 million** "Power for All" batteries sold by early 2023. — [Bosch press release, 10 Jun 2022](https://www.bosch-presse.de/pressportal/de/en/maximum-added-value-for-users-three-new-partners-for-the-power-for-all-alliance-241984.html). Later Bosch pages also list Bosch Home Appliances and HK Audio, and say one battery powers "around 100" alliance products. — [Bosch DIY Power for All page](https://www.bosch-diy.com/gb/en/landing/power-for-all/power-for-all)
- [SOLID] **CAS (Cordless Alliance System, led by Metabo)**: launched in 2018 with "a handful of brands". Finnish Exact Tools became the **50th partner on 8 Apr 2026**. — [Torque Expo](https://www.torque-expo.com/node/20992). Partner literature cites "over 450 machines" and "over 45 brands" in the 18 V class (older material says 25 brands and 280 machines). — [TRUMPF CAS brochure](https://Www.trumpf.com/filestorage/TRUMPF_Master/Products/Power_Tools/Brochures/TRUMPF-battery-machines-CAS-DE.pdf)
- [SOLID] **Einhell Power X-Change**: "one battery for more than 350 tools". The platform was 54% of 2025 group revenue (EUR 1,157.7 m). — [Einhell, 21 Apr 2026](https://www.einhell.com/investor-relations/financial-news/corporate-news/detail/EQS-News_188704911_en/)
- [WEAK] **User complaints**: lock-in through deliberately incompatible interfaces and protocols. One retailer blog claims replacement batteries earn 30-40% margins versus 15-20% on tools (unverified). Third-party cross-brand adapters exist (e.g. DeWalt→Ryobi), but users report problems, such as a pack bricked by an adapter's quiescent drain. Professionals complain about rising pack prices (e.g. Makita). — [FridayParts blog](https://uk.fridayparts.com/blog/how-to-deal-with-the-power-tool-battery-standardization); [ToolGuyd reader question on adapters](https://toolguyd.com/reader-question-battery-adapter-for-milwaukee-ridgid-ryobi-power-tools); [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/threads?id=ashbee); [OzBargain CAS discussion](https://www.ozbargain.com.au/node/894736?page=1)

### Inferences
- What users value in these ecosystems is backward compatibility over decades (Ryobi since about 1996, Bosch 18 V since 2007) and buying "bare tools" cheaply once they own batteries. Anodyne's "ships without cells" model is the same "bare tool" model, which is already familiar to tool buyers.
- The alliances show that brands will share a battery. But every successful alliance is anchored by one dominant battery maker (Bosch, Metabo) that controls the pack and earns from it. An open, unowned standard has no such anchor. The UBS concept needs a business reason for incumbents to join.
- Anodyne's screwdriver and power bank compete directly with Einhell, Bosch, and Ryobi bare tools, which have enormous scale advantages.

### Gaps
- Could not verify current tool counts for Makita LXT, DeWalt 20V MAX/18V XR, Milwaukee M18, or Greenworks within budget. These are often quoted as hundreds of tools each, but I have no source.
- No reliable size estimate for the cross-brand battery adapter market (it is mostly Amazon/AliExpress no-name sellers).
- No current product count for Power for All (the most recent figure found is "around 100", from 2022).

## 3. Loose-cell enthusiast market (flashlights, cells, chargers)

### Takeaway
A mature, price-competitive enthusiast ecosystem already exists. Genuine name-brand unprotected 21700 cells cost about EUR 4-5 each in Europe. Protected or branded cells from flashlight makers retail at USD 25-30, which is 5-6x more. Fake capacity claims are endemic. That makes verified health data (Anodyne's NFC idea) a real selling point, but the niche itself is hard to size.

### Cited Findings
- [SOLID] NKON (Netherlands) sells genuine **Molicel INR21700-P45B (4500 mAh, 45 A, unprotected)** at **EUR 4.55**, falling to EUR 4.25 (30 units) and EUR 4.05 (100 units). — [NKON listing](https://www.nkon.nl/rechargeable/li-ion/21700-20700-size/molicel-inr21700-p45b-4500mah-45a.html)
- [SOLID] Flashlight-brand protected/branded 21700 cells: **Fenix ARB-L21-5000** list price USD 33 (about USD 27-28 on sale), about DKK 159 (≈EUR 21) in Denmark. **Nitecore NL2150HP** at USD 28.95. — [CopQuest](https://www.copquest.com/fenix-arb-l21-5000-21700-rechargeable-battery_18-8505.htm); [Knivesandtools DK](https://www.knivesandtools.dk/en/pt/-fenix-arb-l21-5000-21700-li-ion-battery-5000-mah-XX-50362.htm); [Battery Junction](https://www.batteryjunction.com/products/nitecore-nl2150hp-21700-battery)
- [SOLID] Fake cells: forum tests report "fake" 18650s delivering a tenth or even a thirtieth of the claimed capacity. Real 18650s top out around 3,500-3,600 mAh, so "9,900 mAh" claims are plainly fraudulent. Tester HKJ uses discharge tests to identify rewraps (e.g. "99.5% surely a rewrapped NCR18650GA"). Some flashlight brands (e.g. Sofirn) sell rewraps of other makers' cells, and the cell under the wrap can change over time. — [BLF "9900 mAh for real?"](https://budgetlightforum.com/t/4-pack-of-18650-batteries-with-9900-mah-for-real/70839); [BLF review requests for HKJ](https://budgetlightforum.com/t/review-request-for-hkj-rechargeable-batteries/34262?page=26); [18650 Battery Store authenticity guide](https://www.18650batterystore.com/pages/how-to-spot-fake-18650-batteries)

### Inferences
- The price gap between a EUR 4.50 bare Molicel cell and a EUR 20-30 branded protected cell shows that a brand premium for protection, branding, and trust already exists and is accepted. A UBS cell with NFC health tracking could plausibly sit at EUR 20-35. This is my inference.
- The enthusiast community (BLF, r/flashlight, r/18650masterrace) is knowledgeable and price-sensitive. It distrusts rewrapped cells sold at a markup, so Anodyne would need to name the cell supplier openly.

### Gaps
- No source for the size of the loose-cell enthusiast market (unit volumes or revenue), or for subscriber counts of the relevant subreddits.
- Did not retrieve Battery Mooch's own test posts directly (search returned only secondary mentions). No data on the share of fake cells on Amazon/AliExpress.
- No sourced pricing for Samsung SDI, LG, or Sony/Murata cells, Vapcell, or Wurkkos/Sofirn branded cells.

## 4. Devices using standard removable cells, and the shift caused by EU rules

### Takeaway
The EU Batteries Regulation requires user-removable portable batteries from February 2027. But smartphones and tablets that meet the Ecodesign longevity and water-resistance rules are exempt, so it will not bring back removable batteries in phones broadly. The rule pushes towards *replaceable* batteries in general appliances, not towards *standard* cells. Removable-battery phones remain a niche (Fairphone sold about 150k devices in 2025).

### Cited Findings
- [SOLID] Article 11 of the EU Batteries Regulation (2023/1542) requires portable batteries in appliances to be removable and replaceable by end users. It applies from **February 2027**. Smartphones and tablets covered by the Ecodesign regulation are exempt if they meet battery-longevity and waterproofing requirements, so they "may continue to be sold with built-in batteries". — [Right to Repair Europe](https://repair.eu/?p=7509); [Vienna.at](https://www.vienna.at/new-decision-by-the-eu-not-all-devices-need-removable-batteries/10325174); [ComplianceGate](https://www.compliancegate.com/?p=31689)
- [SOLID] Samsung Galaxy XCover7 Pro: 4,350 mAh removable battery and seven years of updates. It is one of very few mainstream phones with a removable battery. — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/samsung-galaxy-xcover-7-pro-3544249); [Notebookcheck review](https://www.notebookcheck.net/The-all-round-improved-outdoor-phone-with-a-removable-battery-Samsung-Galaxy-XCover7-Pro-review.1031126.0.html)
- [SOLID-ish] Fairphone sold 103,053 phones in 2024 (Impact Report 2024). In 2025 it neared 150,000 devices against a target of 126,000 (may include Fairbuds). — [Fairphone community forum, citing the Impact Reports](https://forum.fairphone.com/t/fairphones-sold-2013-2025/63123?page=4)
- Both phones use proprietary-shaped packs, not standard cylindrical cells. [Inference from product descriptions above]

### Inferences
- The EU rule helps the *narrative* (repairable, replaceable) but does not mandate standard cell formats. Device makers can comply with proprietary removable packs. UBS cannot rely on regulation alone to create demand.
- Small devices that already use loose 18650/21700 cells (flashlights, some power banks with 18650 bays, bike lights, vapes) are the natural first beachhead, because users there already own cells and chargers.

### Gaps
- Could not verify specific product lines of 18650-bay power banks (XTAR, Nitecore), bike lights, or vapes within budget.
- No data on how many device categories will actually redesign for Article 11 by 2027.

## 5. Previous attempts at universal or swappable battery standards

### Takeaway
Swappable standards succeed when one strong operator funds the infrastructure and others license it (Gogoro). Multi-OEM consortia tend to move slowly, taking years from agreement to products (the Japanese SBMC, Honda MPP). Open interface standards like EnergyBus became formal standards but were not widely adopted by the dominant e-bike brands. The lesson for UBS: a spec alone does not build an ecosystem; a product line that uses it does.

### Cited Findings
- [SOLID] **Swappable Batteries Motorcycle Consortium (Japan)**: set up in April 2019 by Honda, Kawasaki, Suzuki, and Yamaha. Agreed standardised swappable batteries and swap systems on 26 March 2021. — [Honda newsroom](https://global.honda/newsroom/news/2021/2210326eng.html). Expanded to 21 members including KTM and Piaggio, with technical specs agreed and prototyping started. It became a liaison member of CEN, CENELEC, and ETSI. — [MCNews AU](https://mcnews.com.au/?p=404724)
- [SOLID] **Honda Mobile Power Pack e:**: about 1.3 kWh (50 V, 26.1 Ah), about 10 kg. Used with Honda's "exchanger" vending stations. Honda aims to make it a light-equipment industry standard used by other OEMs as well. Reported plans for B2B swap commercialisation from June 2026. — [Honda PH](https://www.hondaph.com/motor/mobile-power-pack-e); [Electrek](https://electrek.co/?p=466774); [Visordown](https://www.visordown.com/news/new-bikes/honda-reveals-electric-scooter-standardised-battery)
- [SOLID for 2021 figures] **Gogoro**: as of April 2021, 370,000 riders, more than 175 million swaps (265,000 per day), and about 2,000 GoStations in Taiwan. Licensed its battery to other OEMs through "Powered by Gogoro Network" (Yamaha, Aeon, Hero MotoCorp, Yadea, eReady). Expansion to China was delayed in Nov 2022 by "economic and geopolitical uncertainty". — [Wikipedia: Gogoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gogoro)
- [WEAK] Claims of 12,000-13,000 GoStations and 400,000-450,000 swaps per day in 2025 come only from template or aggregator sites. They are not verified. — [businessmodelcanvastemplate.com](https://businessmodelcanvastemplate.com/products/gogoro-swot-analysis)
- [SOLID] **EnergyBus**: an open standard (connector plus CAN-based communication) for light electric vehicles, charging up to 1.5 kW. Its communication was adopted by the IEC in July 2019 as IEC TS 61851-3. The intended benefit is one charger for batteries of different makers and chemistries. EN 15194 (pedelecs) refers to EN 50604-1, which refers to IEC TS 61851-3-2. — [Wikipedia: EnergyBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EnergyBus); [EnergyBus standards](https://energybus.org/standards). CONEBI (the European bicycle industry association) commented in 2024 on an EU study of common charging for light means of transport, a sign that common LMT charging is still being debated rather than settled. — [CONEBI comments, Nov 2024](https://www.conebi.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/CONEBI-Comments-on-LMT-Common-Charging-Study_20241126.pdf)

### Inferences
- Gogoro's success came from a closed, vertically integrated network that it later opened to licensees. The tool alliances succeeded the same way. "Open from day one, no owner" has a weaker track record.
- EnergyBus has been a formal standard since 2019, yet major e-bike systems (Bosch, Shimano, etc.) still use proprietary batteries. This is the closest analogue to UBS's risk: being standardised without being adopted. This is an inference; I did not check adoption figures directly.

### Gaps
- No primary source on how far EnergyBus has been adopted by e-bike makers.
- No consumer-electronics universal battery standard attempt was found within budget.
- No current (2025-26) primary Gogoro figures (annual report not fetched).

## 6. Consumer demand signals

### Takeaway
Europeans strongly support repairability in principle: about 8 in 10 want devices to be easier to repair and about 77% prefer repair to replacement. But support roughly halves when it would raise the price. Willingness to pay a premium is real but limited.

### Cited Findings
- [SOLID] Eurobarometer (2020, 27,498 respondents in EU27 and the UK): **79%** think manufacturers should be required to make digital devices easier to repair or to replace individual parts such as batteries. — [Restarters summary of the Eurobarometer](https://talk.restarters.net/t/survey-8-in-10-europeans-think-digital-devices-should-be-easier-to-repair/2613)
- [SOLID] Support drops to about **4 in 10** if easier repair means a higher price. — [Lewis Silkin, summary of the Eurobarometer](https://brands.lewissilkin.com/archive/2020/11)
- [SOLID-ish] **77%** of EU consumers would rather repair than buy new. Almost 6 in 10 would pay more for products that are easier to repair or recyclable (2024 Eurobarometer, as reported). — [Lewis Silkin repair tag](https://adlaw.lewissilkin.com/tag/repair)
- [SOLID] BEUC published a "Repair Market Check" report in 2026 (not read in detail). — [BEUC X-2026-066](https://www.beuc.eu/sites/default/files/publications/BEUC-X-2026-066_Repair_Market_Check_Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Stated preference is strong, but revealed preference (sales) is modest. Fairphone sold about 150k devices in 2025, a tiny share of the EU phone market. A premium "repairable" brand should plan for a niche buyer.

### Gaps
- No survey specifically on interest in *standard, swappable cells shared between devices*.
- No Norway-specific survey data found.

### Takeaway
The price ladder Anodyne would sit on: bare quality cells at about EUR 4-5, branded protected cells at about EUR 20-30, good 4-bay chargers at about EUR 25-30, and premium power banks at about EUR 180-230.
